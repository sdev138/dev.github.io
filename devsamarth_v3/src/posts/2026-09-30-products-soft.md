# Products are the New Projects

Having been born and raised in the Bay, I have seen firsthand the rise and integration of a large white collar workforce, different from the blue star employees of Hewlett Packard (now known as HP), Intel, and Lockheed Martin.

This new generation, were tackling problems in mostly one realm, **Software**. Everything was and had to be about the **Software**. Software was the moat, software was the unkillable skill. Everything was going to be software. Every product, every service, distribution, all of it will be on the web. We would have AI's that could do the manual, blue-collar work for us. So construction, plumbing, all of it will be taken over by software. Software would never be able to do any white collar work, white collar (not just technical) work requires a set of **human** skills that no machine can acquire and no software can replicate. Problem solving, connections, subject expertise, etc. Software was the end all be all, but to build effective, scaleable software, required a set of tools, languages, and frameworks to make things possible.

As frameworks and projects became more prevelant, we saw people begin to form a specific skillset arount those specific skills and languages. Someone became a Java expert, and another a Cloud guru. These skills would then be on display through projects, things that you can show a recruiter or your peers, to show mastery in said skills and subject.

However in this new AI era, AI has shown phenomenal progress in almost every subject. People who may not have any expertise in any subject, are now able to perform at an adequate level, that drives values and helps companies reach their OKRs. Whats' the point of mastering every single esoteric function in RxJava, when a curious person could just ask an LLM (any model past Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.5, I've tried with those models, never got the best results at the time)?

Software, although considered to be art, has **literally** become art in the AI era. People will always have a seed of doubt in their mind when viewing art, "Is this AI?" "Has this been generated?".

> In the AI era, Honesty, Accountability, and Credibility are placed at a higher standard than ever before

Just like art, if someone is able to generate slop software that looks good, what's to say the software you're looking at has been generated? And how can you tell that it demonstrates a mastery of their skills?

> Software is not a moat anymore, but at the same time its harder than ever to create a product that people would use, its harder than ever to create value.

## TLDR

Companies are trying to get leaner. Long are the days of overhiring and talent saturation, if you are not developing the product, or generating revenue for the company then you are effectively useless. Their won't be any middle manager, because their won't be enough people to manage. The amount of value an employee can bring to a company will become ever greater, and will only continue to improve.

> So how do you show value in this age?

You've guessed... its to build products, to have users, and even better, to generate revenue. Because if you can show you can go from 0 → 1 from nothing, then you have a greater chance of stepping into any commericial institution and go from 100 &rarr; 1000. Now, are open-source projects that have a lot of stars and users detrimental? Absolutely not, a product doesn't have to be something someone pays for, its only a product if someone uses it. The new economy isn't about showmanship or how you look anymore, its not about wearing an office shirt, a suit, and pretend to work. Its about getting customers, driving growth, and maintaining excellent relationships with your customers.

**Build products, not projects**
